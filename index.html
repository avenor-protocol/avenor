The problem

People find an opportunity in one app
and have to act on it in another.

01

Fragmented journeys

Users find opportunities on X, Telegram, and Discord. Then they have to leave, find the right dApp, and figure out which function to call.

02

Simple actions are hard

Tipping or donating still means copying a 42-character address and choosing the right network. Mistakes are irreversible.

03

Builders repeat work

Every project rebuilds its own landing page, wallet connection, and transaction flow.

Links are a trust problem

Our first product

Cync puts a payment in a link.

Make a transfer, tip, or donation link and share it. Whoever opens it sees what it does, connects a wallet, and signs.

For creators

  1. Choose an action: Transfer, Tip, or Donate
  2. Fill it in: recipient, token (USSD by default), title, image, and a fixed amount or suggested amounts
  3. Sign the link if you like, so payers can see who made it. Any later edit breaks the signature.
  4. Share it. On X, Telegram, and Discord it shows up as a preview card.

For users

  1. Open the link to see the full recipient address, who signed it, what the recipient gets, and the fee
  2. Connect a wallet, in the browser or through WalletConnect
  3. Review and sign. Cync checks and simulates the transaction before your wallet asks.

Why Sonic

The right network for one-link actions.

Solana showed with Blinks that action links work. EVM chains, Sonic included, still have no maintained product for them.

EVM-compatible

Standard wallets, tooling, and Solidity contracts work as they are.

Low fees, own stablecoin

Small payments like tips stay cheap, and they can be paid in USSD, Sonic's native stablecoin.

Fee Monetization

Sonic gives apps a share of the network fees they generate, so this income grows with usage.

Builder ecosystem

Sonic's DeFi and app teams are natural integration partners.

Architecture

Your keys never leave your wallet.

Cync builds the transaction and you sign it in your own wallet. The server can't sign for you, so nothing moves without your approval.

OffchainAction API

Reads the action from the link, checks the creator's signature, builds the unsigned transaction

OffchainAction Viewer

Checks every transaction against the link, simulates it, connects the wallet

UserYour wallet

You review and sign here. Nothing else can sign for you.

Onchain · SonicCync Router

Pays the recipient, takes a fee capped at 1%, never holds your funds

Signed links

Creators sign every field of a link with their wallet, including contract wallets. Payers see who made it, and a signed link that was edited is refused.

Transaction checks

The Viewer rebuilds what the link describes and blocks anything else: another recipient, another amount, or an unlimited approval.

Action Registry v1.5

An onchain record of each action's creator, metadata hash, and verification status, so users can tell real actions from fake ones.

FAQ

Common questions.

What is Cync?

Cync turns a transfer, tip, or donation into a single link. Whoever opens it sees what it does, connects a wallet, and signs.

Can Avenor move my funds?

No. The Action API only builds an unsigned transaction. You sign it in your own wallet after reviewing it, and nothing else can sign for you. Always read what your wallet shows.

Is Cync live?

Yes. Cync is live on Sonic mainnet at cync.avenor-protocol.com. A demo with free test tokens is also available at testnet.cync.avenor-protocol.com; get them from its faucet page. Testnet tokens have no real value.

What does Cync cost?

A percentage of each payment, capped at 1% by the Cync Router. The payer pays the amount shown, and the fee comes out of what the recipient receives. You see both numbers before you sign. The fee on Sonic mainnet is 0.5%. The testnet demo charges 1%.

What does "Signed by" mean on a link?

The creator signed the link with their wallet. You can see which address made it, and any edit to the link breaks the signature. It tells you who made the link, though, and nothing about whether they're trustworthy. Every link is marked "Unverified" until the Action Registry arrives in v1.5, so always check the recipient address.

Is there an Avenor token?

No. Avenor has no token and no plans for a token generation event (TGE). Treat any token, airdrop, or sale that claims to be Avenor's as a scam.

Where are the contract and audit details?

The mainnet and testnet addresses are in the Architecture section above. We'll publish audit reports here once an audit is complete.

How do I avoid fake links and scams?

Only trust links shared from our official channels: this website (avenor-protocol.com), the Cync app (cync.avenor-protocol.com), its testnet demo (testnet.cync.avenor-protocol.com), X, Telegram, and GitHub. We will never ask for your seed phrase or private keys.
